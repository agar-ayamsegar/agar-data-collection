Before placing the fan

Ammonia rises. The smell that stays low is the gas under the chicken.

Ammonia weighs 17 g/mol against 29 for air, so in still air it drifts up. Most of the other spoilage gases (amines, hydrogen sulfide, the acids behind “asem”) are heavier and settle slowly. At the tiny amounts a chicken gives off, warm air and the MQ heaters mix things more than weight does, but in a still box it takes minutes.

What really stays at the bottom is the part of the chicken that touches the floor. No air reaches that skin, and the drip that pools there holds a lot of ammonia, because ammonia dissolves easily in water. That gas leaks out slowly and unevenly, so two runs of the same chicken can read differently depending on how it was lying.

The fix is a rack before any fan: a stainless grid 2–3 cm above the floor, with a washable drip tray under it. Then air reaches the underside, and a gentle fan can carry that gas to the sensor.

Rules that keep the ratio honest

Same fan state in baseline and sampleMoving air cools the MQ heater and changes the reading by itself. If the fan runs during the sample, it runs the same way, at the same speed, during the baseline.

Never blow straight at the sensorsPut them in the return path of the loop, where mixed air passes at a gentle, steady speed.

In a tube, the fan goes after the sensorsThe air reaches the sensors before it touches the motor, which is warm and can carry its own smell.

Purge through a carbon filterThen every baseline starts from the same clean air, whatever the room smells like. Carbon doesn't remove humidity, so keep logging the DHT11.

New box = new dataset versionTag the JSON with "method": "box_v2_D" (or similar) and run the first few chickens in both boxes, so old and new ratios can be compared.

Chicken in the same spot, every timeCentre of the rack, same side up. Mark the spot on the grid.

Recommended for data collection now

Design D: hold, mix, flush

It keeps the strong signal of your current closed-box method (the 5-minute hold), adds a short mix so the sensor reads the average air instead of whatever drifted past, and flushes itself with filtered air so you never have to open the lid to purge. Later, the through-flow design (C) is the one that matches the handheld sniffer; run a few chickens in both to learn how the two relate.
